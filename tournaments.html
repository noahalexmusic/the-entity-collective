New to Competitive Dead by Daylight?

How DBD Tournaments Work.

Dead by Daylight is an asymmetrical multiplayer game: four Survivors face one Killer in each match, known as a Trial. Unlike a traditional 5v5 esports game, the two sides have different objectives.

The Survivor Side — Four Players

Survivors cooperate to repair five generators, power the exit gates, and escape. They must also evade the Killer, rescue teammates, and manage their time. A competitive Survivor team is judged according to the tournament's published scoring rules.

The Killer Side — One Player

The Killer hunts the four Survivors, interrupts generator repairs, and attempts to sacrifice them on hooks before they escape. Tournament scoring may consider kills, hook stages, or other clearly defined performance measures.

An Example Team-versus-Team Match

From Trial to Result.

One common way to organize a team matchup is to give each team a four-person Survivor lineup and a designated Killer player. The sides compete in separate Trials and their performances are compared using the same published scoring system.

STEP 01

First Trial

Team A's four Survivors play against Team B's designated Killer.

STEP 02

Switch Sides

Team B's four Survivors play against Team A's designated Killer, under comparable conditions.

STEP 03

Determine the Winner

Staff compare verified results under the event's scoring and tiebreak rules. The winning team advances if the event uses a bracket.

Important: This is an educational example, not TEC's finalized rulebook. Dead by Daylight tournaments do not all use the same format. Some use kills and escapes, others use hook stages or points. Maps, permitted perks and items, Killer selection, tie-breakers, disconnects, and rematches must be specified before an official TEC event begins.
